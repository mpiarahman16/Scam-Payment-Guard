The problem

In authorized push payment scams, the customer sends the money themselves, so traditional fraud controls don't fire. Banks respond with blanket warnings on every transfer, and customers learn to tap through them without reading.

Design decisions

  • Friction only where risk is. Low-risk payments see no warning at all, which keeps the real warnings credible.
  • Name the scam. The intercept describes the specific pattern the customer is in, in plain words, instead of a generic caution.
  • Ask, then pause. Three yes/no questions break the scammer's script. Any "yes" stops the payment; all "no" still adds a short cooling-off pause.

What I'd measure

  • Scam losses per 10,000 payments, by scam type
  • Stop rate: share of intercepted payments the customer cancels
  • False positives: intercepts on payments later confirmed genuine
  • Guardrail: completion time and drop-off for low-risk payments stay flat
